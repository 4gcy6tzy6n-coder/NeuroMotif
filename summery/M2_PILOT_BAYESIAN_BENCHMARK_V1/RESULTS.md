# M2 pilot Bayesian benchmark

## Result

The benchmark compared no-feedback and fixed action-feedback controllers with an exact hidden-Markov observer for the synthetic process. The observer explicitly models the joint latent target and persistent artifact and is given the target-switch hazard, sensor-noise level, and artifact transition process. The prior project summary reports that this observer achieved higher accuracy than both simple controllers in all nine initial-sweep hazard/noise conditions.

This is a strong task-specific reference for the tested generator. It shows that the observed gains over the no-feedback controller do not establish that the feedback rule is the best way to solve the constructed task. The oracle's privileged knowledge makes it inappropriate to call this a matched-capacity architecture comparison or a general AI benchmark.

## Interpretation and limits

- The result concerns synthetic episodes generated by the project code, not animals or neural recordings.
- The independent unit is the episode. The same simulated episode is evaluated by all controllers, enabling paired comparisons; time steps are not independent samples.
- The benchmark is conditioned on known generator parameters. It does not test estimation under model uncertainty, and it is not a learned neural network.
- This result supports a limitation claim about the initial task family, not biological mechanism validation or general superiority of Bayesian methods.

## Reproducibility

The archived episode table, condition summary, and manifest are copied byte-for-byte from the local run. The public scripts differ from the source only in routing rerun outputs to a fresh timestamped directory in the repository. The manifest records seed, task conditions, oracle knowledge, and the episode-level unit, but does not record a historical Python/NumPy version or dependency lock.
